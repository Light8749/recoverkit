To win back a customer who bought once and went quiet, message them at about the time they'd normally buy again, remind them of what they bought, and make the next purchase one tap away. Timing matters more than the offer.

## Why one-time buyers matter

A customer who has bought from you once already trusts you with their money. They know your site, their details are saved, and they've seen that you deliver. Bringing them back is usually far cheaper than finding someone new through ads.

The mistake most businesses make is waiting. By the time someone notices a customer has stopped buying, months have passed and they've found another vendor.

## How to tell someone has "gone quiet"

"Gone quiet" depends on what you sell:

- **Airtime and data:** many people buy weekly or monthly. Two or three weeks of silence after a regular pattern is a signal.
- **Cable TV and electricity:** tied to a monthly cycle, so a missed renewal date is the clearest signal there is.
- **Fashion, gadgets or food delivery:** longer gaps are normal. Compare each customer with the time most customers take before buying again.
- **Subscriptions:** a failed renewal or a cancelled plan.

A simple rule to start with: if a customer hasn't bought in twice their usual gap between purchases, they're going quiet.

## What to say

Make the message about them, not about you:

- Mention **what they bought**, so they remember you.
- Make **reordering easy**: a link to the same product or plan.
- Give a **reason that's true**, like new stock, a better plan, or a faster delivery area.

For example:

> Hi Bola, it's been a while! Your last DStv Compact renewal was in March. If you'd like to renew this month, it takes under a minute here, and you can pay from your wallet.

Keep it friendly and short. If you'd be embarrassed to send it to a customer in person, don't send it on WhatsApp.

## Regulars who stop are different

If someone bought every month for a year and suddenly stopped, that's a warning sign. Something may have gone wrong: a failed order, bad delivery or a price change. Ask them directly, and listen:

> Hi Emeka, we noticed you haven't topped up with us in a while. Did something go wrong? Reply here and we'll sort it out.

These replies are often the most useful feedback a business gets.

## Measure what actually comes back

Count a customer as recovered only when they make a real, confirmed purchase within a few days of your message, not when they open it or click. That keeps you honest about what's working. It also shows which messages and channels bring people back, so you can do more of what works.
