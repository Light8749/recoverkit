The best way to recover an abandoned checkout in Nigeria is one short, personal WhatsApp message sent a few hours after the customer left, written in your normal voice and aimed at the reason they stopped. Not five messages, and not a discount by default.

## Why Nigerian checkouts get abandoned

Abandoned checkouts happen everywhere, but some causes are especially common here:

- **The payment failed.** Card declined, bank OTP never arrived, or a USSD session timed out. Many customers assume they're the problem and quietly leave.
- **Data ran out or the network dropped** in the middle of paying.
- **Delivery cost or timing surprised them** at the last step.
- **They wanted to ask a question first** about size, stock or delivery to their area, and there was nobody to ask.
- **They were comparing prices** across Instagram vendors and other stores.

Notice that most of these aren't "the customer changed their mind". They're friction. A good follow-up removes the friction.

## When to send it

- **A few hours later** is usually right for small purchases. It's still fresh, and you're not pouncing while they're still on the page.
- **The next day** works for larger purchases, where people take time to decide.
- **Never in the middle of the night.** Schedule messages for normal waking hours, Lagos time.

One message is the default. If you send a second, make it days later and make it genuinely useful, for example to say the item is back in stock.

## What to say

A message that works has four parts:

1. **Their name**, so it's clearly for them.
2. **What they left**, in plain words.
3. **The likely obstacle, removed.** "If your card didn't go through, you can also pay by bank transfer."
4. **One easy next step**: a link straight back to their checkout.

For example:

> Hi Chidi, you were checking out the black sneakers (size 43) earlier. If the payment didn't go through, you can also pay by transfer, and we deliver to Ikeja tomorrow. Here's your cart.

Write the way you'd talk to a customer in your shop. Light Pidgin is fine if that's how your brand speaks. Avoid "URGENT", fake countdown timers and lots of emojis: they read as spam, and on WhatsApp people block spam.

## Should you offer a discount?

Not by default. If every abandoned cart gets 10% off, customers learn to abandon carts. Use a discount only when price was clearly the problem, or for a customer you've already messaged once.

## WhatsApp, SMS or email?

- **WhatsApp** gets read fastest by most Nigerian customers. For business messages to people who haven't messaged you recently, you need the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) and an approved message template.
- **SMS** reaches people without WhatsApp or with no data. Keep it short, and include your business name, since sender IDs vary between networks.
- **Email** suits bigger purchases and business customers, and costs the least.

## Respect consent

Only message people who gave you their number or email as part of signing up or checking out. Every message should make it easy to stop, and you should honour that straight away. The Nigeria Data Protection Act expects you to use personal data for clear purposes and to delete it when asked.

## Don't chase people who already paid

Some customers pay by transfer, or finish on another device, and your checkout never finds out. Before sending, check your payment records again. Only trust confirmed payments from your payment gateway or your own server, never a "thank you" page alone.

## How RecoverKit helps

RecoverKit spots people who started a checkout and never paid, using signed webhooks from Paystack, Flutterwave and other gateways to know who really paid. It drafts a follow-up in your voice for you to approve, and sends it by WhatsApp, SMS or email. It also checks again that nobody has paid before anything goes out.
